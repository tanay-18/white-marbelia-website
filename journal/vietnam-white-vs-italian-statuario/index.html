Vietnam White or Italian Statuario? How two whites differ

Both are white marbles. They create very different rooms. A side-by-side look at character, veining and where each one works best.

Published November 18, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026

People often arrive asking for “white marble” and leave having chosen between two very different stones. Vietnam White and Italian Statuario are a useful pair to compare, because they sit at opposite ends of what white marble can be: one quiet, one expressive.

At a glance

Vietnam White Italian Statuario White
Origin Northern Vietnam (commonly the Yen Bai region) Carrara district, Apuan Alps, Italy
Ground Bright white, often with soft clouding Bright white
Veining Faint grey, often sparse or cloud-like Bold, irregular grey veins with strong contrast
Effect in a room Light, calm, continuous Focal, graphic, architectural
Best suited to Large floors, walls, whole-room schemes Feature walls, vanities, statement surfaces, book-matching

These are typical characteristics. Every slab differs, and appearance varies by quarry and lot.

Character

Vietnam White is about light. Its veining is low in contrast, so from a few metres away it reads as an even white surface with a subtle texture. That makes it forgiving across large areas: a hall, an open-plan floor, a run of bathroom walls. It lets furniture, art and architecture take the lead.

Italian Statuario White is about the stone itself. Its veins are the reason it is chosen, and they change dramatically across a slab. Used on a wall behind a bath or a reception desk, it becomes the defining element of the space.

Planning and selection

With Vietnam White, the priority is consistency: making sure slabs and tiles across a large area sit within a range you are happy with, and distributing any stronger pieces evenly.

With Statuario, the priority is composition. Decide where the strongest veins should fall, whether to book-match, and how veins cross joints. This is best done on photographs or in front of the actual slabs, with the layout drawn before cutting.

Origin and names

Statuario’s reputation means that the name is sometimes applied to similar-looking marbles from other places. If true Italian origin matters to your project, ask for it to be documented. Equally, “Vietnam White” is a commercial name used for stone from more than one quarry, so two lots under the same name can look different. In both cases, the answer is the same: approve the lot, not the name.

Using them together

The two can work well in one project: Vietnam White as the calm field on floors and secondary walls, Statuario reserved for one or two moments where a statement is wanted. Because both have grey rather than warm veining, they sit comfortably side by side.

Which should you choose?

  • If you want the room to feel bright, restful and unified, start with Vietnam White.
  • If you want a single surface people remember, start with Statuario.
  • If you want something in between, look at Namibia White for softer, warmer movement, or Greek Thassos White for near-uniform brilliance.
